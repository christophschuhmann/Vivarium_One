Research and calibration

Use Bennington town Census data, not the county or only the CDP. Household composition changes apply to newly generated towns; existing families are not silently rewritten. US NIMH and SAMHSA references guide a broad condition catalog, with sampling assumptions exposed. The sample is fictional, and overlapping survey estimates are not added as independent diagnoses.

Local activity inspiration: college organizations and senior-center programs. Private Sim identities, schedules and stories are fictional.

Three authoring roles

Dr. Well is a read-only, optional guide available from major screens, with typed and spoken input. It retrieves the current Sim, household, relationships and documented experiences. It explains PERMA, self-determination, coping, bids, response styles and support without pushing cheerfulness or diagnosing the player. It can discuss difficult behavior frankly.

GM uses the same scoped search tools and proposes explicit world edits. Edits are validated, reviewed and logged as interventions, never disguised as remembered events. Ledger transfers remain balanced and age/consent constraints remain enforced.

Storyteller starts with anchored Sims, immediate relationship neighbors and co-present residents. Secondary characters get compact recent context; targeted tools retrieve older memories, professionals, places, relationships and causal evidence. Additional narrative focus requires a stated reason; relevance does not fabricate a friendship.

Memory and private notes

Keep immutable raw events and each Sim’s interpretation. At completed-day boundaries, anchored Sims receive separate factual and personal summaries: approximately two-hour blocks for yesterday, compact day summaries for earlier days and week summaries for older weeks. These are bounded, evidence-linked extracts; BM25 and time-range retrieval keep original details accessible. Catch-up occurs when a Sim is newly anchored.

Storyteller notes are a separate, auditable author-only field for secrets, foreshadowing and unmodeled or magical possibilities. A note alone never causes a hidden physical effect. GM and Dr. Well may inspect notes as out-of-character context; no other Sim gains that knowledge automatically.

Player-facing checks

World monitor: current class bands, financial strain, wellbeing distributions and ongoing interactions, with direct links to Sims and locations. Explorer shares those filters. Crowded scenes use visible sprite pixels for selection, a bounded cast composition and double-click access from the cast rail.

Validate on isolated copies: multiple days across ages and household types, balanced accounts, no mass homelessness cascade, varied PERMA trajectories, support that takes time, treatment access, current—not historical—interaction filters, cross-world tool isolation, bounded retrieval loops, no changes from Dr. Well, memory provenance, and desktop/mobile browser behavior.

Research references

  • Census — ACS 2020–2024: 6,358 households, 2.25 persons per household, median household income $59,446, poverty 13.2%, median gross rent $1,133; town, not county or CDP.
  • Households — Historical household composition is a calibration reference, not a current exact count. New synthetic towns target about one third one-person households, with single-parent and shared homes.
  • Nimh — US population estimates include differing survey years; conditions overlap. Anxiety, depression, PTSD, OCD, bipolar disorder, personality conditions, ADHD and eating disorders have separate pages.
  • Bpd — Emotion-regulation difficulties and treatment; the label does not imply abusive behavior or criminality.
  • Nsduh — 2024 NSDUH, ages 12+: 16.8% past-year substance-use disorder, 9.7% alcohol-use disorder. These are overlapping conditions, not rates of daily use. Treatment receipt is much lower than need.
  • College Clubs — Sports, arts, cultural/affinity, volunteer/academic and special-interest organizations inspire fictional game schedules.
  • Senior Programs — Local senior programming includes walking, painting, games and social support, often free or low-cost.
  • Perma — PERMA as separable building blocks, not a diagnostic happiness score.
  • Sdt — Autonomy, competence and relatedness; distinguish intrinsic aims from externally controlled status pursuits.
  • Bids — Turning toward, away and against bids for connection; describe observable behavior, not deterministic divorce forecasts.
  • Ratio Caution — Brown, Sokal and Friedman: the proposed universal critical positivity ratio is unfounded. Counts are descriptive, never a magic flourishing threshold.
  • Responses — Capitalization and active constructive responding: evidence concerns response patterns, not fixed personality types or guaranteed outcomes.
  • Psychotherapy — Treatment depends on individual difficulties and evidence; CBT is one approach, and care does not equal instant cure.

Implemented and checked

The implementation includes the fixed Dr. Well / Town pulse controls, bounded shared query tools, reviewed GM changes, one-hop narrative fields with extra batches, separate author notes, evidence-linked memory tiers, varied life circumstances, prospective household costs, expected rental income after estimated tax, income/current-interaction filters, real community attendance and sprite-alpha hit testing.

Two rounds of procedural review covered 100 residents for three days and a full week. The final three-day review found 19 attended and 5 missed community sessions, 32 symptom-noticing episodes, 51 episodes of coping with pressure and 43 deferred bill payments. All sampled age groups retained bounded needs and the complete town had no repeated interruption/restart loops. Aggregate PERMA in the final sample ranged from about 41 to 82, with individual dimensions lower or higher. Some Sims remained secure and connected while others had recurring budget deficits or emotional difficulty.

Read representative personal journals. The saved machine-readable review includes distributions, event counts and contrasting residents. These tests do not prove all possible stories are plausible. Condition-specific patterns are deliberately simplified game heuristics, not clinical trajectories; prevalence weights are approximate overlapping US references, not claims about local residents. The larger cast retains its pre-existing families.

A 1,000-resident benchmark executed all 1,440 minute ticks in 259.7 seconds, producing 194,155 events and 212,588 personal records. Process memory peaked near 1,274 MiB and the scratch database occupied 922 MiB. This is a measured single-day result on the development machine, not a forecast for millions of Sims. Raw reversible history remains the main long-run storage cost; memory summarization reduces model context, not stored evidence.

Automated checks cover ledger balance, forbidden/foreign edits, Dr. Well’s lack of write capability, lookup limits, narrator retrieval, one-hop selection, anchor catch-up, summary idempotence, export/import, age limits, 60 single-minute steps versus one hour, and the existing 15-suite regression set. Chromium checks cover an 18-person scene, transparent overlapping sprites, cast-rail double-click, desktop/mobile advisor context and monitor navigation. A real configured-provider Dr. Well query successfully searched professions and household records on a database copy.

Physical state remains procedural and authoritative inside each committed minute. Narration can interpret and guide the future, not rewrite a previously paid bill or completed journey. GM changes require the existing Apply action and remain explicitly authorial. Query tools use a provider-independent JSON request protocol, at most four rounds of four lookups, with bounded output—not unrestricted SQL.
