Bennington: a minute-by-minute living town

Implementation plan · 7 October 2026. This document describes the requested engine makeover. A feature is not considered delivered until its implementation and validation are recorded in the verification report. Existing worlds retain their identity and history; Bennington is a separate scenario.

1. The simulation contract

One tick is one simulated minute, for every Sim, including unanchored residents. Fast-forward is a sequence of these same ticks. A long task has a planned duration, accumulated progress, an interruption policy and a resumable identity. Every minute still checks arrivals, messages, obligations and urgent needs. Expensive replanning happens when something relevant changes. Named seeded random draws depend on world seed, Sim identity, minute and decision type, rather than the length of a UI request.

PhaseBehavior and invariant
Time and resourcesUpdate needs and due obligations once. Work, purchases, care, education and financial contracts use their existing validated operators. No payment is duplicated by an interruption or resumption.
Progress and movementAdvance tasks and routes; resolve actual arrivals and capacity. Retain earned progress when an activity is paused. Replanning cannot teleport a Sim.
Local interactionBuild location occupancy once. Candidate contacts are local and bounded; known relationships and shared goals influence selection. Simultaneous proposals cannot reserve the same person or resource twice.
Immediate feedbackApply accepted social outcomes, needs, expectations and wellbeing before the next minute. Rejection remains possible. A conversation can interrupt research, lead to disagreement and influence later decisions.
Personal historyJournal task starts, interruption and its cause, resumption, completion, arrivals, messages, conversations, outcomes and meaningful changes. Each entry separates objective facts from the participant's subjective perception, uncertainty and emotional interpretation. No mandatory empty entry each minute.
StorytellerRun at bounded scene boundaries. Supply real events, goals, needs, finances, relationships, ToM and personal memories. Validate proposals before they affect subsequent minutes. Physical and financial facts cannot be rewritten after they have caused later events. Preserve anchored Sims, anchored locations and their actual contact fields.
Long advancesProcess long advances in bounded, committed intervals with progress, cancellation and clear partial completion. A day comprises 1,440 minute ticks. Never present one coarse update as a complete simulated day.

2. Personality, motivation and social feedback

Retain OpenSims' Big Five, ambitions, preferences, skills and percentile checks. Expose them coherently. Add shared-goal recognition between known people, invitations, cooperation, help requests, encouragement, appreciation, disagreement, repair and boundary-setting. Extraversion changes contact initiative; introversion is not a deficit. Ambition strength and prosocial/self-interested strategies change action weights. Poor wellbeing may increase withdrawal or irritability, but never determines a person's behavior.

Model active constructive, passive constructive, active destructive and passive destructive responses as conditional probabilities, with relationship-specific learning. A genuine supportive response may strengthen positive emotion and relationships; dismissal may create disappointment or withdrawal. Advice, counseling and coaching offer opportunities to practice communication, not guaranteed happiness or instant personality conversion. PERMA is five partly independent dimensions, not a diagnosis or moral ranking.

Shared goals need concrete opportunities: compatible schedules, reachable places, willingness, skills and resources. Family care, a work project and a shared hobby can all supply such opportunities. A lack of shared hobbies is not an automatic relationship penalty. Record what each person has actually learned; inferred motives remain beliefs with confidence, not omniscient facts. Public reputation is transmitted through supported encounters or news, rather than magically known everywhere.

Adult first impressions use varied individual preferences for appearance, warmth, reliability, competence, status and compatibility. Population averages from research do not become universal rules for men or women. Attractiveness is neither consent nor emotional closeness. Status and partner competition can produce insecurity, boasting, rivalry, generosity, repair and boundary-setting. W100 cannot override another person's refusal.

Age rules remain hard invariants. Romantic affection is zero below 14. Ages 14–17 have a moderate cap of 0.35 and only nonsexual, age-appropriate romance with a maximum one-year age gap. Sexual interactions involving anyone under 18 are neither simulated nor narrated. Adult sexuality is separate and consent-gated. These checks apply to procedural selection, model proposals, interpretation and imported content.

3. Bennington, Vermont

Use Bennington town as the geographic reference, not an asserted recreation of every resident. Begin with a representative synthetic population and benchmark 1,000–2,000 Sims before enabling larger scenarios. Calibrate age cohorts and household forms against Census town data, while keeping parents, children, education and employment mutually plausible. Include older people living alone, supported care, family caregiving, students, unemployed adults and varied resources.

Use real public streets and public institutions such as Bennington College, the public library and civic landmarks. Private businesses and every resident are fictional. Neighborhoods, streets, buildings and rooms form the existing collapsible circular World graph. Public venues have appropriate rooms and schedules. Fictional industrial crime and mystery seeds are marked as fictional scenario material; rumors stay distinct from established facts.

The new scenario uses US dollars and explicitly simplified regional economic rules. Existing euro worlds retain their currency and history. Household classification is calculated from equivalized disposable income with a displayed reference and separate wealth/liquidity information; it must not infer worth, trustworthiness or social standing from class. Preserve jobs, applications, qualifications, employer impressions, rent, ownership, taxes, care, benefits, loans, arrears, scarcity and municipal services as one balanced ledger.

4. Incremental Hugging Face library

Download immutable revisions of both supplied datasets, verify shard hashes and retain source/license metadata. Import only actual image records into SQLite FTS5 with BM25; planned but ungenerated images are not available assets. Respect current release filters and withdrawn pixel hashes. Index age, gender, ethnicity, clothing/style, setting and expression separately. Search can exclude identities already used in the selected world.

Keep original WebDataset shards on disk. Create browser previews and keyed character sprites on demand and cache them. Expressions share an identity, so emotional changes select a compatible variant. The browser keeps its thumbnail budget; the library page uses pagination. Synchronization is repeatable and imports new uploads without regenerating towns. Exported used assets carry provenance and applicable license text.

5. Readable, explainable play

English becomes the default interface and new narrative language. Preserve source text and existing histories. Provide translated display labels and a clear language control; native English scenario text avoids requiring thousands of paid translations. Keep Play, Mind conversations, interventions, World, Bonds, Cast, anchor navigation and replayable TTS compatible.

Turbo and longer lives

The Play toolbar now provides a Turbo dialog with an explicit explanation before starting. Durations range from one hour to 365 days. All Sims, including anchored ones, use the same minute rules; there are no Storyteller calls. A background worker commits hourly checkpoints and reports progress. Closing the browser leaves it running. Cancellation keeps completed checkpoints; a server restart stops, rather than silently resumes, the job. Independent worlds may use up to two workers, while a single world preserves sequential causal minute boundaries.

TransitionImplemented decision and consequences
Aging and educationBirth dates advance real integer age at the relevant calendar day. New enrollment is distinct from graduation. Observed qualifications require learning and attendance; turning 18 never supplies a job or money. Teen romance pauses at an adult/minor age boundary while friendship and factual history remain.
Working lifeExisting job applications evaluate skills, credentials, expectations and supported impressions. Financial pressure, unpaid wages and sustained ambition can motivate search. Firms may lay off workers. The simplified retirement threshold is 66; it is a game parameter, not a statement about local law. Earned wages survive an ended contract.
PartnershipUnrelated adults can start dating after reciprocal attraction and trust. Sustained conflict for at least two weeks can cause a separation or divorce event; it is not triggered by one bad mood. Neither dating nor sharing a home grants intimate consent.
Household and housingAdults can form an independent household after an affordable accepted offer. Rejections keep the previous home. Established compatible one-person households may agree to combine. A split does not seize shared money, relocate children, transfer old debt or teleport anybody. Existing rental/eviction and funded housing-support rules remain active.
ScarcityThe dominant physical need selects an appropriate resource and destination. A refrigerator does not substitute for a water source. If food is unavailable, that constraint is recorded once and checked again without fabricating repeated successful activity. Youth access to funded meals is age-appropriate; young children retain caregiver routing restrictions.

Readable multiday journal audit: sampled lives, actual event descriptions and personal interpretations. Performance reports live in the repository's artifacts/expanded-world directory. Month/year controls run the full minute engine; a complete year and populations above 2,000 have not been validated. Long histories consume disk space, so partitioned/compressed history and synchronized region processing remain necessary before city-scale expansion beyond this release.

6. Delivery and verification gates

  1. Back up the current database; capture baseline regression results.
  2. Implement resumable tasks and causal event journals; test completion, interruption, return, cancellations and save/load.
  3. Apply social effects within minute boundaries. Test seeded short/long procedural equivalence, privacy, age rules and accounting invariants.
  4. Integrate downloaded assets, structured search, expressions and safe incremental reindexing.
  5. Generate Bennington as a separate scenario; validate demographics, household consistency, reachability and workplace assignment.
  6. Integrate social dynamics, English surfaces, learning cards and profile/navigation improvements.
  7. Benchmark populations in stages; record CPU time, memory, events and journal volume. Do not claim 10,000-Sim performance without measured evidence.
  8. Run browser checks and real scenario inspection. Document delivered behavior and remaining limits, restart the expansion server and push the tested branch.
